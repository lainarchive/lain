# 07 — Implementation Sequence

**Status:** Proposed plan. **No step in this document has been executed.** Every "files expected" entry describes a *future authorized implementation*, not work done.
**Base revision:** `36a3e18003ad41171be96d28cfbbda5b5304441c`
**Labels:** ESR · UAP · OQ · EUR · M/D/P as established.

**Ordering principle (ESR).** Deterministic, verifiable, model-free work first — because it can be completely proven on this machine today, and because every later capability records through it. Nothing in Phases 5–8 is started before its shared foundations exist.

## Step 4 — Policy decision contract

- **Objective:** a total `decide()` with eight arms, default `deny`, and `deny_indeterminate` distinct.
- **Prerequisites:** step 2 (decisions are recorded events).
- **Files (future):** `src/policy/{decide,arms,approvals}.mjs`, a decision-table test fixture covering all arms.
- **Acceptance:** every arm reachable and tested; no request yields an exception; no request yields `allow` by default; an approval timeout produces `deny_indeterminate` and is **not** retried (positive control: a `deny` **may** be re-requested after a documented change).

**ESR — why M-2 is gated and not merely sequenced.** Every M-2 step depends on a measurement that has not been taken (P-5) or an environment that has not been exercised (a real model call at 4 096 context). Starting it earlier would mean writing code whose central assumption is untested — the exact pattern the audit criticized in the original roadmap, which scheduled the riskiest assumption last.

## Sequencing risks, stated honestly

1. **The largest risk is not technical.** M-1 produces no visible capability; its value is that later claims become checkable. If the goal shifts to "something that runs today", every step after 1 becomes negotiable — and the negotiable ones are exactly the ones that prevent silent corruption (ESR).
2. **Step 0.1 is the only step that edits canonical documents** and therefore the one most likely to be deferred. Deferring it means the repository keeps asserting that mods use infrastructure which does not exist and that execution is container-isolated — claims a future contributor will build on.
3. **Step 2's byte-scan redaction check is the cheapest insurance in the plan** and the easiest to skip under time pressure.
4. **P-5 is the highest-value open measurement** and blocks M-2.2 entirely.
